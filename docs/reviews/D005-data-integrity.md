# D005 independent perspective: data integrity

Adviser: `/root/data_integrity_adviser`. Recorded 2026-11-02 UTC. This is an independent recommendation, not a panel consensus or a certification of an unimplemented system. No credentials, paid providers, service processes, or market orders were accessed. Only this review file was written.

**Recommendation: build the US-equity acquisition and provenance boundary before admitting real-data studies, while keeping raw acquisition useful even when research eligibility is blocked.** The user selected both asset classes with US markets first. Use an explicitly selected historical Alpaca SIP feed if the owner's account actually has access; require `adjustment=raw`, a fixed symbol-mapping policy and completed historical intervals. Build the artifact and research gates so later providers and asset classes fit the same evidence contract. Exclude Coinbase from this recommendation following the separate source review's terms finding.

The current engine already enforces positive OHLC values, unique increasing sessions, one symbol, consistent timezone, raw-price treatment and next-supplied-session-open fills. It rejects supplied corporate actions and checks an optional caller-supplied session list. However, its report correctly labels exact availability as unverified and its holdout as unprotected. The journal can register exact specifications, data identities and code identities and retain invalid, rejected and failed attempts. The missing component is an application boundary that makes those identities and eligibility checks mandatory before a study executes.

The following requirements determine whether the next increment adds credible evidence rather than merely more prices:

1. **Preserve an acquisition receipt and exact raw responses.** Record provider, endpoint, non-secret request parameters, requested instrument/window, feed, adjustment and symbol-mapping settings, request/response times, response status, page sequence, byte counts and SHA-256 identities. Include the reviewed rights basis, terms reference/date and allowed use; technical access alone is not a rights determination. Keep raw payloads outside Git. Persist raw artifacts by content hash; a later response must create a new version rather than overwrite the bytes behind an earlier study. Record parser/normalizer version, canonical normalized-data hash and a link to every contributing raw page. A file hash establishes identity, not truth or storage immutability.

2. **Separate the clocks.** A market session or bar interval, a provider's publication/revision time, and the local acquisition time are different facts. Store interval start/end and session timezone separately from `retrieved_at`; retain source publication times only when supplied and understood. Do not assign historical `available_at` from the observation date, midnight session label or today's download time. A historical download without recorded historical versions is a retrospective snapshot, not proof of what a live strategy could have received then. Alpaca documents late bar updates and trade corrections, which makes revision identity consequential. [Alpaca real-time stock data](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/real-time-stock-pricing-data)

3. **Make coverage independently checkable.** Record requested and returned bounds, page exhaustion, expected session schedule identity, observed sessions, missing sessions, duplicates, conflicting duplicates, partial bars and out-of-range records. Do not infer completeness from a successful HTTP response or a full first page. Alpaca states that the limit covers all symbols together and that pagination can initially contain only one symbol. Normalize ordering explicitly, deduplicate identical page overlap with an audit count, and reject conflicting observations rather than silently taking the latest row. [Alpaca historical bars](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars)

4. **Treat missing sessions as a research eligibility failure unless the absence has a documented explanation.** A Monday-to-Friday generator is not a US-equity calendar. Pin the session schedule and its source/version, including early closes and timezone rules; identify whether bars represent regular or extended trading. A supplied calendar can establish a specific comparison but is not proof that every absent instrument bar is a provider fault: listing dates, suspensions and feed coverage also matter. Do not forward-fill, shift the trade to an arbitrary later supplied row, or splice feeds to make the study pass. [NYSE trading hours and calendars](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars)

5. **Replace implicit corporate-action absence with an explicit claim and evidence.** The existing empty `corporate_action_events=()` argument cannot distinguish “checked and absent” from “never queried.” Admission needs a declared action-coverage state, relevant window, source and retrieval/version identity. Unknown coverage must not become an empty event set. Alpaca's action endpoint can omit incomplete records by default, sorts by `process_date`, and explicitly does not guarantee creation timing; a narrow query or no returned rows cannot automatically certify absence by ex-date. Until split/dividend accounting is implemented and independently reconciled, a study intersecting relevant actions should be rejected by the research boundary. Raw acquisitions can still be retained. [Alpaca corporate actions](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/corporateactions-1)

6. **Record instrument identity and selection scope.** Tickers are labels that can change or be reused. Store provider instrument identity where available, symbol-mapping policy, currency, venue/feed and explicit universe-selection date/rule. Alpaca's `asof` parameter controls symbol mapping; it is not a general historical knowledge-time filter. A handful of current symbols supports only the declared fixed-universe study. It does not establish a delisted-inclusive, survivorship-controlled market warehouse. [Alpaca historical bars](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars)

7. **Freeze the study before calculating or exposing evaluation results.** The registered specification must reference exact accepted dataset/manifest, code and calendar identities, instrument selection, costs, execution assumptions, chronological windows and declared selection process. Record every attempted study and failure. Keep holdout exposure in a durable registry tied to instrument/time coverage and research lineage; giving revised data or a modified strategy a new hash must not erase prior exposure. Until enforced access and exposure handling exist, use an explicit exploratory/unprotected status and refuse a protected-validation verdict. “Later dates” alone do not establish an untouched holdout.

The source choice does not require a paid live-data plan for every historical use. Alpaca's current FAQ says historical SIP queries can use an `end` at least 15 minutes old without a subscription, whereas live IEX covers only one exchange. That supports checking the owner's authorized historical access before buying anything. It does not establish this account's access, dataset rights, full market coverage across all historical listings, or the adequacy of next-open fills. Select and record the feed explicitly rather than relying on account-dependent defaults. [Alpaca Market Data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq)

Next-open execution remains a modeling assumption until the source's aggregation scope and availability are established. A daily aggregate's open must not be relabeled as a regular-session opening-auction fill without supporting evidence. A completed signal must be available before the assumed execution point, with an explicit latency convention. This becomes especially restrictive for later continuous markets, where the close of one time bucket and the open of the next share a boundary; changing the asset class does not create time to observe, compute and execute.

For later fundamentals, preserve filing/accession identity, the economic reporting period, publication evidence and amendments separately. SEC frames select a last-filed fact matching a calendar period; using today's frame as a historical signal can therefore introduce later information. SEC company facts and submissions provide useful free building blocks, but assembling point-in-time features remains our responsibility. [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) Macro features need the same distinction: FRED's default describes information known today, while ALFRED-style real-time parameters retrieve historical vintages. A period date is not its release date. [FRED real-time periods](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/realtime_period.html)

The implementation acceptance test should show one real authorized US-equity acquisition with a reproducible manifest and quality report, including a deliberately rejected invalid/unsupported study retained in the journal. Independent tests should cover truncated pagination, conflicting duplicate bars, a missing session, a changed source revision, unknown or present corporate actions, and later information entering an earlier decision. Then execute a registered historical study only if its declared eligibility conditions are actually met. Do not weaken those conditions to obtain a green end-to-end demonstration.

This increment should leave the broader warehouse requirement open. Multi-decade delisted coverage, point-in-time universes and fundamentals, action-complete portfolio accounting, empirical execution costs and prospective paper evidence still need their own sources, implementations and verification. The decisive tradeoff is less immediate backtest output in exchange for results whose data and assumptions can later be audited.
