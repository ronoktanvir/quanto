## Recommendation

Proceed with a small US-first acquisition/provenance slice and an all-attempt exploratory study workflow using fixed common evaluation windows. The owner selected US markets and crypto, with US first; this increment should acquire one narrowly bounded US daily-bar sample, then make normalized bars eligible for deterministic exploration only when their provenance and completeness checks pass. Add crypto as a separate adapter after the US acquisition contract is demonstrated. Do not describe the resulting evaluation period as a protected holdout.

The efficient operational boundary is one explicit, historical, read-only Alpaca request path: daily bars, exact start and end, `feed=sip`, `adjustment=raw`, and an explicit symbol mapping/as-of choice. The official Alpaca documentation says historical SIP queries may be available without a paid subscription when the requested end is at least 15 minutes old; its stock-bars API also exposes pagination and adjustment/feed parameters. This is a documented possibility, not confirmation that the owner's account is entitled to the data or that its terms permit every planned storage and model use. The documentation's default feed depends on account access, so the request must specify the feed rather than inherit that default. ## What the inspected implementation supports

The research function is a calculation boundary. It validates one-symbol, ordered, positive OHLCV bars; accepts only raw prices; rejects supplied corporate-action events; can compare rows against a caller-supplied session schedule; and labels exact availability and holdout protection as unverified/unimplemented. It compares the strategy and benchmark over the same post-warmup bars. Those constraints are useful for an exploratory workflow, but they do not establish a real source, currency, session calendar, corporate-action absence, or provider entitlement.

The journal already offers useful durability primitives: canonical JSON and SHA-256 identities, transactionally coupled event and record writes, a hash-chained event log with an explicit external-checkpoint limitation, and append-only application records for experiments, attempts, outcomes, and reviews. It records failed, invalid, and rejected attempt outcomes. `overview()` exposes attempts lacking outcomes. These pieces make an auditable workflow possible, but no acquisition module currently joins them to HTTP, raw artifacts, pagination, normalization, or a research run.

There is a practical schema mismatch at the acquisition boundary. `start_attempt()` requires a registered experiment fingerprint; an acquisition request begins before response bytes and the resulting data identity are known. Reusing the strategy-experiment record with a placeholder data hash would blur request identity with dataset identity and create a second fingerprint when the data arrives. Add a small acquisition-run record keyed by a canonical request manifest, or an equivalent type-specific attempt record, instead of overloading strategy experiments. Keep the existing registered-experiment path for studies, whose exact data identity is known before calculation.

## Minimum reliable acquisition slice

1. **Preflight access and scope.** Require credentials through the existing ignored local configuration, but never print or persist them. Keep entitlement and rights review as explicit states supplied by the owner; a successful HTTP response is not a rights decision. Fix one symbol, timeframe, feed, adjustment, mapping/as-of rule, currency expectation, and historical date interval. No subscription purchase is needed for this proposal.
2. **Commit the request attempt before sending.** Persist the canonical non-secret request manifest, implementation/parser identity, start time, and unique attempt ID before the GET. Store safe query parameters rather than a credential-bearing URL. This records failures such as DNS errors, authorization denial, timeouts, malformed JSON, rejected coverage, and process interruption.
3. **Bound the request.** Use GET only, fixed dates, a modest explicit response page size, a small page ceiling, a total byte ceiling, and a finite timeout. Follow returned page tokens until exhausted or until a declared bound is crossed. Do not retry automatically or switch feed, endpoint, symbol mapping, or date window. A later deliberate retry is a new uniquely identified attempt linked to the earlier failure.
4. **Preserve bytes and page lineage.** Store each exact successful response outside Git under its content hash, with a canonical manifest containing provider, endpoint, safe query, requested and returned bounds, feed, adjustment, as-of/mapping rule, retrieval UTC times, HTTP status, page ordinal/token lineage, byte count, response hash, and parser/normalizer version. Deduplicate storage by content hash without overwriting prior observations. A digest establishes byte identity, not provider truth, retention immutability, or rights.
5. **Validate before publishing normalized data.** Require complete pagination, expected response structure, one requested symbol, monotonic session timestamps, valid OHLCV, no conflicting duplicates, no out-of-range rows, and explicit units/currency/time zone. Record validation failures as outcomes while retaining raw evidence. Do not infer a market calendar from weekdays, silently fill missing rows, or silently substitute a different feed. If the applicable session calendar or action coverage is unknown, retain the acquisition but block it from any claim beyond exploratory raw-price calculation.
6. **Reconcile interruption without erasing ambiguity.** If the process dies after the attempt record, leave a visible unfinished attempt. On recovery, record an `interrupted`/`unknown` outcome (or equivalent explicit state) rather than pretending the provider definitely failed; a crash can occur after a successful response and before its manifest is committed. Reconcile any already-written content-addressed blobs by hash. Any new GET uses a new attempt ID. Never auto-repeat a paid model call as part of acquisition recovery.

Alpaca's historical endpoints document that pages can be partial and expose a next-page token; therefore, HTTP 200 is not a completeness check. A total bound may intentionally reject a large request, which is preferable to an unbounded crawl. [Historical stock bars pagination](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars)

## Explorer and evaluation boundary

Register a study only after normalized data and its manifest have stable identities. Every submitted specification should produce an attempt record before calculation and a terminal success/invalid/failed/rejected outcome afterward. Preserve rejected studies and exceptions. The explorer should bind candidates to the same predeclared chronological evaluation dates after warm-up; where lookbacks differ, specify a common warm-up policy and common scored dates so the benchmark and candidates are compared over identical windows. The date-window rule and candidate-search procedure belong in the experiment identity.

These windows remain exploratory. Hashes and durable attempt records improve reproducibility and search accounting; they cannot prove the owner or agents had no prior exposure to historical dates, nor can they establish complete capture of trials outside the application. Therefore use `unprotected`/`exploratory` labels and keep the protected-holdout feature explicitly pending. Do not let a new strategy hash, data revision, or changed window erase prior recorded exposure.

## Cost and reliability tradeoff

Alpaca historical GETs have no model-token cost under the cited documented plan, but still consume account access, bandwidth, local storage, and operator time. Cost control should come from a narrow date range, one symbol, small page limits, bounded bytes, no retries, and private content-addressed storage. Do not route raw licensed market data to outside-model APIs. Existing local USD 20/day reservations apply to cooperating outside-model clients, not Alpaca data requests or unrelated clients of the account; there is no reason to charge a provider request to that ledger as if it were an Anthropic call.

Keep SQLite claims precise. The ledger code itself says its event chain is tamper evidence, not immutability; ordinary append-only triggers do not prevent a database owner from rewriting the database. A trusted external head checkpoint and backup/restore test would strengthen tamper detection, but should not delay the bounded acquisition slice. Similarly, reliable unattended research scheduling, stale-worker fencing around provider work, backup restore, and host-level monitoring are separate milestones, not implied by adding an HTTP client.

## Dissent and unresolved limits

My main caution is against making the first real-data slice a warehouse project. A single-symbol, fixed-window acquisition is enough to exercise provenance and failure paths; expanding into broad symbol universes, delisted coverage, corporate-action accounting, fundamentals, and crypto normalization before that path is reliable increases cost and delays learning. That narrow sample is also not representative market coverage and cannot support a strategy claim.

The reverse risk is calling the proposed explorer a completed research protocol. The current engine's explicit unsupported areas include corporate actions, session calendars, availability timing, and holdout exposure. Raw closes omit dividends, and the supplied next-session open is a model assumption rather than proof of executable timing. A successful acquisition plus deterministic output can validate data plumbing and calculation behavior only. A strategy evaluation requiring total return or protected selection must wait for action accounting, justified calendars/timestamps, and exposure controls.

Crypto is in scope, but its continuously traded session structure, venue/pair identity, currency denomination, and data-rights terms need their own contract. The crypto endpoint also documents pagination; the stock `feed=sip` / `adjustment=raw` parameters do not transfer as if they described crypto. Defer that adapter until the US-first manifest and attempt semantics are stable, then compare only within explicitly defined common timestamps and market rules. [Alpaca historical crypto bars](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/cryptobars-1)

## Decision summary

I support a bounded, no-subscription US historical acquisition followed by a reproducible all-attempt explorer on fixed common dates. First implement the acquisition attempt/artifact contract and its crash semantics; then validate real-data parsing and completeness; then admit exploratory study runs. Keep source entitlement, rights, calendar and corporate-action coverage explicit, and do not market the result as a protected holdout or validated strategy. This sequence spends little, yields useful provenance early, and preserves a truthful path toward the larger operation.
