## Subsequent source-only failure-memory diagnosis

The lead proposed that the two historical D006 refusals were projected as `unresolved` despite D007's later accepted source/policy lineage. This section assesses that hypothesis from implementation and the public D007 report only; it does not inspect private failure or financial artifacts. The already verified exact authorized retrieval shows which approved records were supplied, but does not establish why the hypothesis stopped.

`FailureMemory._disposition` defaults to `unresolved` when no disposition record exists. That means no later claim was recorded, not that the failure has been independently reconfirmed under the current policy. Its latest recorded disposition wins for retrieval. `append_disposition` supports only `unresolved` and `corrected`; it requires an existing failure, a terminal outcome for a corrected claim, an approved code and retained allowlisted fix bytes. The appended receipt is explicitly provisional and leaves the original outcome unchanged. The function checks existence and identity, not causal relevance, repair efficacy, semantic-review acceptance or the identity/authority of an independent reviewer. Those assertions need their own evidence.

The public `D007-historical-pilot.md` documents a successful later policy revision retaining both refused requests and predecessor plans, followed by qualified admission and the registered suite. It explicitly says the earlier refusals remain valid and D007 is a new policy after acquisition. Therefore `corrected` is an imprecise description of the historical outcomes. A relation such as “later admitted under a reviewed policy revision for this exact scope” is more faithful than implying either old refusal was mistaken or its unsupported feature is now universally supported.

There is also a projection limitation: the broker constructs `FailureMemory` without allowed fix-artifact references and forwards only the disposition's `state`. It drops the provisional qualification, disposition code, evidence availability and fix-reference state. Existing tests explicitly show that a corrected claim can remain recorded after its fix artifact disappears, with `artifact_state=unavailable`. Merely appending `corrected` and sending that single word would erase this distinction. The present traces did not use such a shortcut; it must not be introduced to obtain model assent.

A properly sourced current-applicability relation would need, locally:

1. The exact original failed submission/attempt, unchanged outcome/code and exact original request identity.
2. The old versus later policy/source identities, with the later revision's explicit ancestry to the original request and retained intermediate changes. A generic later success is insufficient.
3. The exact successful later admission and suite within the same claimed scope, verified against current retained dependencies and event ordering. Scope outside that verified admission must remain unresolved.
4. A precise claim distinguishing repaired implementation, new policy exception, changed evidence and limited supersession. The claim must retain residual qualifications, not convert conditional retrospective admission into source truth, broker-settlement support, protected status or investment validation.
5. The issuer and any actual independent reviewer identities, exact artifacts inspected, disposition and objections. A registered claim or a content hash is not review certification.
6. A safe projected category that preserves whether the relation is merely claimed, evidence-verified or independently reviewed, and whether its supporting artifact is available. Source identifiers, private requests and underlying data remain local.

This is a plausible operational-memory gap: current retrieval preserves failures but cannot faithfully communicate scoped policy supersession with its two-state field alone. It is not evidence that memory caused either stop, and it does not justify changing the original failure statuses. Prefer a separately typed, exact current-scope relation over overloading `corrected`; review that relation and its egress semantics before using it. No disposition was appended in this review.
